Woodwind fingering lane

Mockup for early learners: the note name over every note (the word the teacher uses in the lesson), a small hole diagram where the fingering changes a lot, colour for the beginner's hurdle, and the full diagram kept on screen in a side bar or a tray. Tap any note, or press Play. Fingerings are standard, drawn schematically; the tunes are traditional.

The side bar

Use it in landscape and on iPad. In portrait, use the tray.

  • Phone in landscape: a 132pt side bar leaves 535pt of staff, about four bars a line. A tray would leave only 179pt of height, about one line of music.
  • Phone in portrait: the side bar leaves 243pt, two bars a line, and the labels crowd. Height is what portrait has spare, so the tray goes there.
  • With the full diagram always on screen, the lane over the staff can stay light: labels, plus a small diagram only on big changes.
  • The lane shows note names. Teachers say “play B” and teach the fingering once, from a chart. Number shorthand appears in charts and guides, not over the music, so here it is an option.
  • “Put down / Lift” is the part a parent can read aloud. It turns the chart into the next instruction.

What to check in the real app

  • The phone already has a bottom tray (playback, chords). The side bar has to share the screen with it.
  • The same panel could show a violin note as a finger on a string. It is one component for the whole family, with a different drawing per instrument.
  • The clarinet's labels and diagrams are in written pitch, as in the child's book. The panel also gives the sounding note.
  • Baroque and German recorders differ on F. Switch the fingering system and play “Right hand down” to see it.
